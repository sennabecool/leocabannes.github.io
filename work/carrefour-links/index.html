What is Carrefour Links case study about?

Oh boy, this one is a banger. Buckle up, it’s going to be a wild ride.

Carrefour Links · 2023

Bridging the Gap Between Retail Category Managers and Brand Product Teams

A 6-month project to centralize category performance, standardize KPIs, and make joint decision-making between suppliers and retailers easier.

Carrefour teams and suppliers were working across separate tools, with no shared taxonomy and no single place to track joint projects.

I led the product design of a web platform used by Category Managers and Key Account Managers to monitor performance, compare results, and follow shared initiatives.

The problem

The issue wasn’t access to data. It was alignment.

Teams had to work across siloed tools, interpret KPIs differently, and manually piece together the information needed to make decisions on topics like promotions or restocking.

We needed to create one place where both Carrefour and suppliers could:

  • • Use the same KPIs;
  • • Understand category performance;
  • • Track joint projects;
  • • Make decisions from the same information.

My role

I was the Lead Product Designer on the project, embedded on-site for six months.

The team included:

  • • 2 Data & AI consultants
  • • 1 Lead Product Designer
  • • 2 developers

The first two months focused on discovery and design, followed by four months of build. I led interviews, UX/UI design, prototyping and user testing, while working closely with data and engineering throughout delivery.

What we focused on

1. Make performance easy to read

We designed the category view around the indicators users checked most often, with the option to break results down by family, supplier, brand, product or format.

The goal was simple: see the signal first, dig into the detail when needed.

The final dashboard combines headline KPIs, filters, comparison tools and detailed performance breakdowns in one place.

2. Connect projects to results

Carrefour and suppliers also needed to know whether their joint initiatives were actually working.

We created a project-performance view where users could compare the selected scope against a reference perimeter and follow its evolution over time.

This made it easier to move from:

“What happened?” to “Did this project have an impact?”

3. Keep expert workflows intact

These users work with a lot of data, so oversimplifying the product would have made it less useful.

We kept advanced controls where they mattered: filtering, comparisons, detailed breakdowns and configurable Excel exports.

The challenge was not to remove complexity, but to make it easier to navigate.

The Result

The final platform brought category performance and joint-project tracking into one shared workspace.
